Step 2 of 4

Give it one job that runs without you

Anything you repeat, however small. This prompt makes Fable find the job, pick it, and build it.

I want you to take one thing off my plate for good.

First, interview me. Ask me up to 8 short questions about my week: what I do every day or every week that feels like a chore, what I copy and paste, what I check, what I remind people about, what I type out over and over. One question at a time. Wait for my answer before the next one.

Then list every repeat task you heard, and for each one tell me two things: how much time it eats per week, and how risky it would be to hand to you (low, medium, high).

Pick the ONE with the best mix of time saved and low risk. Tell me why you picked it.

Then build it. Write me the complete job description I can hand you every single time: what to look at, what to do, what "done" looks like, and where I have to approve before anything goes out. Write it so a brand new assistant with zero context could run it perfectly.

Finish with the plainest possible instructions for making this run on a schedule inside Claude, step by step, so I never have to start it by hand again.
1
Paste it and send. It asks you questions one at a time. Answer honestly, even if the answer is “I don’t know.”
2
It ends with a finished job description and the steps to make it run on a schedule. Save both.

Act like the pickiest client I could possibly land. You have money, you have options, and you have a short attention span.

Go through everything I gave you like you are deciding whether to hire me.

Give me:

Step 4 of 4

Your biggest mountain

The thing you avoid because you don't know where to start.

1
Start a new chat. Type out the whole thing first, as messy as you want. Every detail, every worry, every half idea.
2
Paste this underneath and send.
Above is the thing I keep avoiding because I do not know where to start. Treat it like the most important project in my business.

Before you plan anything, ask me the 5 questions that matter most. One at a time. Push back if my answers are vague.

Then give me the plan:
1. The goal in one sentence, in plain words.
2. The one decision that is blocking everything else, and your recommendation on it.
3. The whole thing broken into 3 to 6 phases, each with a name and what "done" looks like.
4. Inside phase one, the first three steps, each small enough to finish in under an hour. Number them.
5. What I can safely ignore for the next 30 days.
6. A realistic timeline, and the one thing most likely to blow it up.

Keep it short enough to fit on one page. No jargon. If I would need to look up a word, use a different word.

When I say "go", walk me through step one like you are sitting next to me.
3
Answer its five questions. When the plan lands, type go and it walks you through step one.
Confusion creates inaction. This turns the mountain into a numbered list.
